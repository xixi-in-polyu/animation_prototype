# Aesthetic defense

Use this reference for every substantial new animation. The objective is not to ban a style category; it is to prevent an unexamined, interchangeable "AI promo" look from becoming the default.

## Anti-default declaration

Add `antiDefault` to the Reel Read. Name the likely generic treatment for this subject and the concrete decisions that will make this project recognisable without relying on copy or color.

Require each direction to state:

- its composition logic, typographic roles, spatial rhythm, and motion grammar;
- which visible choices come from the benchmark and why they fit the communication job;
- which tempting defaults it deliberately rejects;
- whether the direction remains identifiable in grayscale with decorative effects removed.

Three directions are not distinct when they only change palette, card arrangement, icon set, or transition preset.

## Defaults that require justification

Treat these as review prompts rather than universal prohibitions:

- blue-purple gradients, neon halos, glass surfaces, and soft floating panels;
- a dashboard or rounded-card grid used for content that is not a dashboard;
- decorative orbs, particles, fake depth, and ambient motion with no narrative role;
- centered headline plus symmetric cards as the repeated scene grammar;
- identical spring entrances, staggered reveals, or constant motion on every element;
- generic stock metaphors and interface chrome that could be swapped into another topic unchanged.

Use one of these choices only when the Reel Read or direction rationale explains its communication function. Decoration is not a function.

## Adversarial visual check

During rendered review, ask:

1. Could this frame belong to an unrelated AI-generated product trailer after replacing the words?
2. If color, glow, and rounded containers disappear, is there still a specific visual idea?
3. Does each surface group information, establish hierarchy, or show state?
4. Do motion and transitions express sequence, causality, comparison, or emphasis?
5. Are adjacent scenes genuinely composed, or are they the same template with different content?

Record a concrete correction for every failed answer. Do not claim aesthetic approval from a validator or heuristic preflight.
